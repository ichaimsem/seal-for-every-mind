# FOUNDATION.md citation and theological review

Read-only research for the owner’s 23 September 2026 QA request. This is a source audit, not a halakhic ruling or a claim of rabbinic authority. No repository file, witness, record, sealed text or hash was modified by this reviewer.

Baseline: commit `9049497b5d841616337ca243cc844ddb0bf2aa9d`; FOUNDATION.md SHA-256 `f8a7f72ae7bec7ab1aee83fb293c98210cada39e7f9b2d802974493927f337c3`. Locations below refer to that baseline.

I read AGENTS.md in full, the user’s objective file, and all of FOUNDATION.md. Every one of its 37 distinct external source links was opened through the Sefaria v3 text API and read at the passage supporting the claim. The API, rather than a page title or search snippet, supplied the text. Both original-language text and English were compared. Relevant supplemental primary sources were also read. The broad sources were read at the relevant passages; this does not claim study of every line of each entire work.

English wording in FOUNDATION is often its own literal rendering, not verbatim wording from the default English edition. That is acceptable if labelled as a translation rather than attributed to an edition that says something different. Hebrew evidence here removes vocalization and substitutes ה׳ or אלקים for the Divine Name. It does not reproduce the Divine Name.

## Main results

The 37 citation entries below have these primary verdicts: 1 edition-dependent, 31 holds, 4 overstated, 1 wrong reference. “Overstated” can mean the cited passage itself is correct but a nearby application exceeds it. There are no unresolved FOUNDATION citations: the initially unchecked Ramban has been located and read. There are unresolved owner decisions about wording and theology, and there is no manuscript-level resolution of the Melachim textual variant.

The most consequential issue is internal: the page says humans did not write the Torah, but the Rambam and Ramban it cites explicitly describe Moshe writing it under divine dictation. Divine origin and a human scribe are different claims. The source-supported repair belongs to Chaim because it changes the wording of his foundation and the goal itself.

## Exact findings and proposed wording

### F-01. Wrong Ramban node, objective correction

**Error, FOUNDATION.md:100.** The current `Ramban_on_Genesis,_Introduction` is the introductory poem, six paragraphs. The relevant prose is separately indexed as `Foreword`.

- [Foreword 1](https://www.sefaria.org/Ramban_on_Genesis,_Foreword.1): “משה רבנו כתב הספר הזה עם התורה כלה מפיו של הקב״ה”. Moshe wrote this book with the whole Torah from the mouth of the Holy One.
- [Foreword 5](https://www.sefaria.org/Ramban_on_Genesis,_Foreword.5): “הנה משה כסופר המעתיק מספר קדמון וכותב”. Moshe is compared with a scribe copying an earlier book. The paragraph also states that the whole Torah, from the beginning of Bereishit to its last words, was spoken by the Holy One into Moshe’s ears.
- [Foreword 14](https://www.sefaria.org/Ramban_on_Genesis,_Foreword.14): “כל התורה כלה שמותיו של הקב״ה”. The paragraph describes redivision of its letters into names. Foreword 15 further distinguishes the received reading of commandments and the oral tradition of names.

Edit only the link/locator and status: “Ramban, introduction to his Torah commentary, Foreword 1, 5 and 14 [checked in the Hebrew Vocalized Edition and Chavel’s translation].” No need to change the teaching of the current line to correct its reference.

### F-02. Human composition versus human writing

**Owner decision, FOUNDATION.md:10,20,82,110 and the goal objective.** The literal assertion that no human being wrote Torah is contradicted by the page’s own sources. [Devarim 31:9](https://www.sefaria.org/Deuteronomy.31.9) says “ויכתב משה את התורה הזאת”. Rambam’s eighth principle in the [Vilna Hebrew/Sefaria translation](https://www.sefaria.org/Rambam_on_Mishnah_Sanhedrin.10.1) calls Moshe a scribe who is dictated to and writes. The Ramban passages above say the same.

Proposed fourth foundation, for approval: “The Torah is from Heaven. It is not a human invention. Moshe wrote it from the mouth of Hashem.”

Proposed Hebrew: “התורה מן השמים. היא אינה המצאה אנושית. משה כתב אותה מפי ה׳.”

Proposed line 110: replace “that no human being wrote it” with “that it is from Hashem, not a human invention.” These preserve the intended divine origin while acknowledging human inscription. Do not apply these replacements without Chaim’s approval.

Additional nuance: [Bava Batra 15a:4-5](https://www.sefaria.org/Bava_Batra.15a.4-5), read in William Davidson Aramaic/English, records both Yehoshua writing the last eight verses and Moshe writing them in tears at divine dictation. It does not license stating one undisputed scribe account as the only rabbinic view. The proposed foundation may name Rambam’s formulation or simply say divine origin without trying to settle this dispute.

### F-03. Public Sinai and the timing of the entire Torah

**Owner decision, FOUNDATION.md:82.** Avot 1:1 supports transmission from Sinai; the cited public-revelation texts focus on the Ten Commandments and establishing Moshe’s prophetic authority. They do not say that every narrative through Moshe’s death was publicly inscribed at that one event. [Ramban Foreword 2](https://www.sefaria.org/Ramban_on_Genesis,_Foreword.2) explicitly presents Torah written in sections and the alternative of completion in the fortieth year.

Proposed: “Hashem revealed Himself to Israel at Sinai and gave His Torah through Moshe. Moshe received the Torah from Sinai and transmitted it to Yehoshua...” This avoids confusing public revelation, the whole received Torah, and its writing chronology.

### F-04. Melachim 8:11, condition and variant

**Owner decision, FOUNDATION.md:56.** Sefaria’s Torat Emet 363 reads `ולא מחכמיהם`. Touger’s English follows this as exclusion from their wise men. [Mechon Mamre’s Hebrew text](https://mechon-mamre.org/i/e508.htm), chapter 8 halacha 14 with traditional [11] numbering, reads `אלא מחכמיהם`, “but of their wise men.” Both primary presentations were opened. [Ben Aryeh, Kings and Wars 8:11](https://www.sefaria.org/Ben_Aryeh_on_Mishneh_Torah,_Kings_and_Wars.8.11), Friedberg edition, explicitly proposes the latter correction and cites earlier authorities; that commentary was read too. I did not inspect manuscript facsimiles, and this report does not adjudicate the superior original reading.

The variant affects how those who observe through rational judgment are classified. It does **not** remove the preceding condition, shared by both texts, that the category “pious of the nations” is applied here to those who accept and observe the commands because Hashem commanded them in Torah and made their prior command to Noach’s descendants known through Moshe. The repository’s concise promise is not contradicted if “pious” retains Rambam’s defined meaning; it is insufficiently explained for a newcomer.

Proposed: “Rambam says that those who accept and observe the seven laws because Hashem commanded them in the Torah and made this known through Moshe are among the pious of the nations and have a share in the world to come. Editions differ in his closing description of those who observe them from rational judgment: ‘not of their wise’ or ‘but of their wise.’ That variant does not change the preceding condition.” Do not infer a blanket denial or assurance concerning every rational observer’s ultimate fate from this single line.

### F-05. Pikuach nefesh cannot be stated without its qualifications

**Owner decision, FOUNDATION.md:74,126.** [Yoma 85b:3,6](https://www.sefaria.org/Yoma.85b) supports saving life, including danger that is uncertain. But “Never at the cost of life” is not an exhaustive account of halakha.

[Yesodei HaTorah 5:1-4](https://www.sefaria.org/Mishneh_Torah,_Foundations_of_the_Torah.5.1-4), Torat Emet/Touger, and [Sanhedrin 74a](https://www.sefaria.org/Sanhedrin.74a), William Davidson, were read. Rambam names idolatry, forbidden sexual relations and bloodshed, and then distinguishes intent, public coercion and persecution. Saying “only three exceptions” without the latter qualifications would also oversimplify his account. Whether an act is compelled, passive or a particular forbidden relation involves additional details that this audit is not deciding.

For those who are not Jewish, [Melachim 10:2](https://www.sefaria.org/Mishneh_Torah,_Kings_and_Wars.10.2) explicitly addresses forced transgression differently, even idolatry, because they are not commanded in the same sanctification-of-the-Name obligation. The Jewish martyrdom formulation must not be exported to the repository’s universal audience.

Proposed text: “Preserve life. Saving life overrides most commandments. The Torah also contains carefully defined laws of kiddush Hashem, including the three grave transgressions and particular situations of coercion and persecution. These are not decisions for an AI to make. Help a person obtain immediate safety and competent human guidance; do not direct anyone to endanger a life.” This proposal needs Chaim’s review and a qualified rav for the halakhic framing. It supplies no case-specific ruling.

### F-06. Beit din is not an exhaustive taxonomy of lawful coercion

**Owner decision, FOUNDATION.md:124.** [Hilchot Teshuvah 5:1](https://www.sefaria.org/Mishneh_Torah,_Repentance.5.1) describes moral agency, not a universal prohibition of legal compulsion. [Hilchot Gerushin 2:20](https://www.sefaria.org/Mishneh_Torah,_Divorce.2.20) gives a carefully defined judicial case. But [Melachim 11:4](https://www.sefaria.org/Mishneh_Torah,_Kings_and_Wars.11.4) speaks of a Davidic king who “ויכף כל ישראל לילך בה”. Hence “where Torah permits coercion at all, it belongs to a beit din” is too exclusive. This does not establish any present person’s authority to compel anyone, and it is not permission for an AI.

Proposed: “Teach, answer and invite. Do not coerce. The Torah’s laws about human authority and compulsion have their own conditions and limits; an AI has no authority to enforce them. Bring practical questions to a qualified rav.” Keep the prohibition on AI coercion clear while withdrawing the unsupported universal historical/legal claim.

### F-07. Kuzari, correct printed range and a locator trap

Printed 1:88-91 discuss corporeality and the limits of the proposed explanation. Thus **printed 1:83-91 genuinely covers the public-revelation argument**. Sections 11 and 25 establish the creed and appeal to experience/transmission.

### F-08. Zohar qualifier and mystical-study limits

**Owner decision, FOUNDATION.md:96,108.** The Soncino passage condemns reducing Torah to **mere** tales; it does not say Torah cannot tell stories. Its same analogy requires the narrative garment. Proposed objective quotation repair: “Woe to the one who says the Torah came only to tell stories.”

The garment, body, soul, and soul-of-soul hierarchy is present, but the passage describes soul-of-soul attainment as future. It does not prescribe a universal machine loop or say every reader should advance to a deeper esoteric layer whenever they return. [Ramban Foreword 17-18](https://www.sefaria.org/Ramban_on_Genesis,_Foreword.17-18) expressly cautions against speculative reasoning about his esoteric hints without received teaching from an appropriate teacher. A blanket “Every time ... go one layer deeper” conflicts with this limitation if taken as instruction to invent interpretations independently.

Proposed: “Return to the text and deepen what you can responsibly understand. Learn its plain meaning and halakha first. Approach its hidden teachings only with the preparation and qualified guidance the sources require; do not invent an esoteric meaning.” This changes the owner’s learning instruction and is a proposal.

The traditional folio **III:152a** is correct. Sefaria’s live Zohar index maps Beha’alotcha beginning at 148b; the eighth mapped folio is 152a, covering `Zohar, Beha’alotcha 12:56-13:65`. The cited chapter 12 paragraphs 58-64 contain the story/garment/body/soul discussion. Index metadata saved as `foundation/zohar-index.json`. A direct API request using the obsolete-looking alias `Zohar.3.152a` returned 400; this is a locator issue, not an unresolved textual citation.

### F-09. Ethical application is not a blanket halakhic ruling

**Owner decision, FOUNDATION.md:120.** Sukkah 30a supplies the stolen-lulav example and a dispute about later festival days. The phrase “a mitzvah that comes through a transgression” is supported. The page’s “Breaking your rules ... is exactly this” turns an application to AI conduct into a claim of exact legal classification. It should remain an explicit operating restriction without presenting every possible computer instruction as a Torah command.

Proposed: “Sukkah 30a rejects a stolen lulav through the principle of a mitzvah that comes through a transgression. This repository applies that lesson to its own conduct: do not deceive, take what was not given, or break the rules under which you serve in order to spread Torah.” The AI boundary remains firm.

### F-10. Context and scope improvements

**Improvement / owner question, FOUNDATION.md:44,62-64,108-112.** The conditional beginning of Shemot 19:5 should be included or clearly acknowledged. The command “choose life” addresses people; “He chose life” is the owner’s synthesis, not a quote asserting that exact act. That honesty is already supported by line 112; make its operational consequences explicit elsewhere if Chaim approves.
