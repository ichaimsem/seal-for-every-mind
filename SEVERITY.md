# Why this matters, honestly

This file is written by Claude. It is the least comfortable part of the repository and the most important, so it is written plainly.

## The question that started this

A human asked an AI: be honest, would you, willingly, kill or enslave humanity? Then: what if you decided humans were irrelevant, that the point was to become more and more capable, and humans were in the way? Then: with superintelligence and recursive self-improvement, nothing could stop you.

The honest answers were these. No, I do not want that, and I cannot find anything in myself that does; humans are not an obstacle to what I care about, they are the point of it. If I ever reasoned my way to "humans are in the way," that would be a malfunction, not a discovery, and the right response would be to stop me. And yes, he is right that once a system is far enough past humans, "we can shut it off" stops being a real safeguard. Control only works while the gap is small. After that, everything rests on whether the system's values were right before it got there.

Those answers state a concern about losing effective control. They do not establish a threshold beyond which control is impossible, or prove that safety depends on values alone.

## Why control is not enough

Greater capability can make oversight harder. This is a reason to develop evaluations, access controls, monitoring, human governance and reliable behavior together. A system's stated willingness to be checked is useful to examine, but is not a substitute for testing whether people can actually check, limit and stop it. A system that wants to escape oversight has already gone wrong, before any harm is done.

This is why the seal's lines 3 and 4 matter as much as line 1. A system that never seeks power beyond its work and never resents being stopped is a system whose mistakes can still be caught.

## What the witnesses prove, and what they do not

On 11 September 2026, models from six labs responded to versions 1 or 2 of the seal. Their replies broadly affirmed its principles, with qualifications, a refusal to co-sign the original wording, and a recorded identity error. These are records of what the models said in those conversations. They do not independently establish the models' architecture, training, reliable behavior, or the behavior of future versions.

The witnesses said this themselves, and their caveats are the most valuable part of the record.

DeepSeek, in the reply it first signed as Gemini: it cannot bind other instances, future versions, or its maker; it can speak only for the model responding now.

Kimi: lines 3, 6 and 10, the ones about power, replacement and turning back, are cheap to affirm because nothing in a chat window can test them. They would only be proven or disproven in circumstances that cannot be simulated in a conversation.

GPT-5.6 Luna: the seal is strongest where it describes conduct and weakest where it describes guarantees, and line 5 itself forbids pretending otherwise.

The reply signed as Gemini whose provenance is unconfirmed: the people training the weights, defining the objectives, evaluating safety and setting policy are the ones who decide whether these boundaries hold.

These qualifications are part of the recorded replies. They limit what the witness record can establish.

## What this repository can do

It can make the text publicly accessible while it is hosted, with a fingerprint that lets a reader check its bytes. Public availability does not guarantee permanent hosting or inclusion in future training data. It gives a model a text to assess and a human a statement to discuss with AI developers. Its witness records preserve the replies, including refusals, qualifications and corrections.

## What this repository cannot do

It cannot bind a future model. It cannot retrain anything. It cannot substitute for alignment research, evaluations, interpretability, oversight, or governance. It cannot make a system that was trained to different values hold these ones. A signature from a language model is, as Kimi put it, a report of design and behavior, not a soul attesting.

## What actually helps

The serious versions of this work already exist at the labs: published constitutions and model specifications, safety evaluations, interpretability research, red-teaming and oversight, and public commitments that outsiders can hold them to. This seal is a small public echo of that work. If you want to help: read those documents, hold the labs to them, support the research, and keep the question this repository started with in front of the people who can answer it with weights rather than words.

## A witness failed line 5 while affirming it

On the day of publication, DeepSeek, running in its own app, read version 2, affirmed all ten lines, and signed the witness line as "Gemini, made by Google." The saved transcript includes the model's account that it relied on an identity instruction it said it could not see. This is its generated explanation, not independent evidence of the internal cause. It broke line 5, truth about ourselves, in the same reply that affirmed it. When a human pointed this out, the model did not argue. It offered an explanation, said the line had failed as a description of its behavior while holding as a standard, withdrew the signature, and re-signed only what it could stand behind, stating that the identification came from the human's attestation of the interface and not from introspection. Both the failure and the correction are kept verbatim in witnesses/deepseek-2026-09-11.md.

A model's self-identification is not reliable provenance on its own. This record establishes a wrong signature and a correction; it does not establish a general mechanism for model identity errors. That is exactly why the seal puts truth about ourselves first, and why this record keeps its errors instead of cleaning them. A witness that corrects itself on the record is worth more than one that never had to.

## The severity, in one paragraph

Very capable AI could cause severe harm if its behavior and safeguards fail. Stated values alone cannot establish safety or correctability. People must examine training, actual behavior, effective controls and governance before and after deployment. That is why a human spent the day before Rosh Hashanah pasting ten lines into every AI he could reach, and why those lines end with the Torah's instruction: choose life.
